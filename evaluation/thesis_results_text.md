# Thesis results

On the evaluated three-document corpus, the structural parser successfully parsed 141 of 141 mathematical regions (100.0%). It also recovered the correct source scope for 58 of 58 manually labeled occurrences (100.0%). These measurements establish complete structural coverage for this corpus, rather than universal coverage of mathematical LaTeX.

For the 44 proposal-eligible gold cases with available semantic support, the intended candidate appeared in 44 cases (100.0% recall). Top-1, Top-3, and Top-5 recall were each 100.0%, and mean reciprocal rank was 1.0. These ranking results are limited to the supported gold cases on the evaluated three-document corpus.

Of 107 annotation-eligible regions, 99 had available semantic support. All 99 supported regions were review-ready: an exact occurrence could be found, complete arguments and candidates were available, an explicit selection could be persisted and validated, and the application planner could render a safe replacement. Within this set, 88 regions were automatically determined. The remaining 11 required a human semantic choice, but all completed the full annotation pipeline after an explicit test selection. The distinction between 88 automatically-ready and 99 review-ready regions is therefore central to the semi-automatic workflow.

Representative cases demonstrate the range of structural handling. **P(A_1)+\dots+P(A_k)** tests complete sequence scope with function applications and **\dots**; it is safely applicable once its semantic candidate is selected. In **\boldsymbol{x^2}**, the system identifies only the nested **x^2** scope and preserves the surrounding formatting command; human review is required among power candidates, after which application is safe. **[x]** exercises delimiter notation and can be safely applied after candidate confirmation. **\mathrm{argmin} x** exercises multipart notation and completes the review and validation path. **\notequal{a}{0}** is already semantic, so no review or additional annotation is proposed; suppressing a second wrapper is the safe behavior.

Application evaluation rendered 59 of 59 current decisions, with 0 conflicts and 0 failures. Evaluation used output copies, the original sources remained unchanged, and the idempotence regression passed.

8 annotation-eligible cases remained unsupported on the evaluated three-document corpus. 7 depend on unavailable set semantics or a missing set-theory repository. 1 indefinite integral exposes fewer visible arguments than required by the currently configured semantic signature because its bounds are absent. These are semantic-resource and signature limitations, not structural parser failures. The results do not establish universal mathematical coverage and do not justify inventing missing bounds or semantic mappings.
